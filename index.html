Taking security back in-house: an MSP exit on a 30-day clock

An outsourced IT and SOC provider held admin access across identity, the password vault, device management, the network and monitoring, and its contract was about to end. I led the consulting team that took that access back in one weekend, without an outage and with an evidence trail an auditor can follow.

Context

A mid-sized US company, sister organization of a fintech client, had outsourced IT and security monitoring to one provider for years. The fleet is almost entirely macOS on Apple silicon. New leadership decided to bring security in-house.

The provider contract ended about 30 days after formal notice. Its device-management tenant, monitoring billing and compliance tooling ended with it. I was security lead on a 7-person consulting team in Q4 2026.

The problem

Nobody had the full list

Access had accumulated over years across about 27 business applications, 10 of them core. There was no single record of what the provider could reach.

Order decides the outcome

Rotate credentials in the wrong order and you lock out your own team, or leave a live session on the layer you have not reached yet.

Hidden dependencies

Cutting off a provider also cuts off what it quietly ran: the MDM tenant, log retention and compliance evidence.

How I ran it

  1. DiscoverAudited the shared password vault entry by entry. Years of history were split by period across the team so it finished in days. I used an LLM to categorize the export, then verified by hand, and built the application inventory alongside it.
  2. Access matrixOne sheet, one row per application: provider accounts, roles, how they authenticate, status. It became the single source of truth for the client and the team.
  3. Runbook and GanttA step-by-step rotation runbook with owners, order, rollback and a communication plan. Rule: credential changes are announced to their owners before rotation, never after.
  4. Cutover weekend7 engineers rotated admin credentials and MFA factors in parallel: vault administrators first, then identity provider, network, MFA recovery channels, then SaaS.
  5. Revoke and removeProvider accounts suspended and roles stripped under a 30-day soft-delete policy to preserve audit trails. Legacy remote-management agents uninstalled by MDM script, then verified by policy: 0 hosts remaining.
  6. Monitor and provePost-cutoff anomaly monitoring, a hunt for leftover persistence such as scheduled tasks, and a chain-of-custody log of 19 artifacts with SHA-256 hashes.

Deliverables
Consolidated access matrix, credential-rotation runbook, Gantt, provider deactivation tracker, chain-of-custody evidence log, post-cutover action plan with architecture evaluations.

Decisions after the cutover

Taking the keys back was half the job. Each service the provider had been running needed a new home before the contract ended.

What I would tell the next team

Start with the vault

If the provider can still read the vault, every rotation after it is theatre.

Announce before you rotate

Owners hear about a credential change before it happens. Surprise lockouts cost more trust than they save time.

Soft delete during notice

Suspend and strip roles. Hard deletion waits until the audit trail has been exported.

Minutes after every meeting

In a handover the written record is a control. I sent minutes after every call.
